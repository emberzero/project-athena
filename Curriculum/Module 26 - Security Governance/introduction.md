# Security Governance

Most of this curriculum is about technology: how attacks work and how to stop them. This module is about the organisation around the technology. Someone has to decide how much security a company needs, who is responsible for it, how much money it gets, and how anyone knows whether it works. That is security governance. It decides whether the firewall rules from Module 05 are reviewed every year or forgotten, whether the incident response plan from Module 13 has a named owner, and whether the board finds out about a risk before or after it turns into a headline.

Security governance is also where most security jobs meet law and regulation. NIS2, DORA, the GDPR and the Cyber Resilience Act all demand that organisations manage their risks in a documented way, and standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 and BSI IT-Grundschutz describe how. The module focuses on Austria and Germany and places both in their EU context.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish security governance from security management and describe the roles involved: management body, CISO, information security officer and data protection officer
- Explain what an information security management system (ISMS) is, how ISO/IEC 27001:2022 is structured, and how certification works
- Carry out a qualitative risk assessment: identify assets, threats and vulnerabilities, rate likelihood and impact, choose a treatment and record it in a risk register
- Compare NIST CSF 2.0, BSI IT-Grundschutz and ISO/IEC 27001, and explain which EU regulations (NIS2, DORA, GDPR, Cyber Resilience Act) apply to which organisations
- Write a security policy, plan an awareness programme, and describe how internal and external audits work

## Topics Covered

1. Governance, Roles and the Security Organisation
   - Governance versus management
   - Board, CISO, information security officer, data protection officer
   - Three lines model, reporting lines and conflicts of interest
1. Information Security Management Systems
   - ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 4-10 and the PDCA cycle
   - Annex A and ISO/IEC 27002: 93 controls in four themes
   - Statement of Applicability and certification audits
1. Risk Management
   - Assets, threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood and impact
   - Risk treatment: mitigate, transfer, avoid, accept
   - Risk register and ISO/IEC 27005
1. Frameworks and Regulation
   - NIST CSF 2.0 and BSI IT-Grundschutz
   - NIS2, DORA, GDPR and the Cyber Resilience Act
1. Policies, Awareness and Audits
   - Policy hierarchy and writing a policy
   - Security awareness programmes
   - Internal audits, external audits and compliance in practice

## Prerequisites

- Module 01 (Ethics and Legal), in particular the data protection sections
- Module 24 (Government Agencies), Lecture 2, for NIS2 reporting obligations
- Helpful: Module 13 (Blue Teaming), for incident response and business continuity

By the end of this module, students should be able to read an ISO 27001 Statement of Applicability, run a small risk assessment, and explain to a manager which regulations apply to their company and what they require.
