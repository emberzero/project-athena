# Red Teaming

Modules 04 to 22 teach how individual attacks work. A red team engagement puts them together and asks a different question: if a real attacker with a real goal went after this organisation, would anyone notice, and could they stop it in time? Answering that question safely is mostly a management problem. Someone has to decide what the engagement should prove, which systems may be touched, who is allowed to know, what happens when something breaks, and how the results turn into better defence instead of a report that nobody reads.

This module covers that management and governance side. It does not teach attack techniques, tools or evasion; those belong to the technical modules and to the professionals who carry out a test. Instead it explains how red team engagements are commissioned, regulated, scoped, planned, supervised and closed, with a focus on the European frameworks that apply in Austria: TIBER-EU, its Austrian implementation TIBER-AT, and threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) under DORA.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish a red team engagement from a vulnerability scan and a penetration test, and describe the red, blue, purple and white (control) team roles
- Describe the TIBER-EU framework and DORA threat-led penetration testing, including their phases, the parties involved and the documents each phase produces
- Explain what an authorisation letter and rules of engagement must contain, and how deconfliction, stop conditions and data handling protect everyone involved
- Turn threat intelligence into test scenarios and use MITRE ATT&CK at tactic level as a common language for planning, reporting and measuring detection coverage
- Plan the closure of an engagement: replay and purple teaming, technical and executive reporting, and tracking remediation to the end

## Topics Covered

1. What Red Teaming Is
   - Vulnerability scan, penetration test and red team engagement
   - Objectives-based testing and adversary emulation
   - Red, blue, purple and white (control) teams
1. Frameworks and Regulation
   - TIBER-EU and TIBER-AT
   - DORA threat-led penetration testing
   - CBEST and other national schemes
1. Scoping and Rules of Engagement
   - Authorisation, scope and out-of-scope systems
   - Legal context in Austria
   - Control team, deconfliction, stop conditions, data handling
1. Threat-Informed Planning
   - From threat intelligence to scenarios
   - MITRE ATT&CK as a planning and reporting language
   - Measuring detection coverage
1. Purple Teaming, Reporting and Closure
   - Replay and purple teaming
   - Findings versus detection gaps
   - Executive and technical reports, remediation tracking

## Prerequisites

- Module 01 (Ethics and Legal), in particular the sections on § 118a and related provisions of the StGB
- Module 03 (Penetration Testing), Lectures 1, 2 and 5, for the difference between test types, rules of engagement and reporting
- Module 13 (Blue Teaming), Lecture 1, for the defender's side
- Module 26 (Security Governance), Lecture 4, for DORA
- Helpful: Module 24 (Government Agencies), Lecture 3, for threat actors and how they are tracked

By the end of this module, students should be able to read a red team test plan and rules of engagement, explain to a manager why a red team engagement is or is not the right tool, and write an engagement plan that a control team could approve.
