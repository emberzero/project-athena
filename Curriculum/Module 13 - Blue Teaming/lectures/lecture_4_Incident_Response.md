# Incident Response

## Definition

Incident response belongs in every organization's cybersecurity strategy. It is a systematic framework for how to respond to incidents that may occur.

## Example

Take a data breach as an example and follow it through the phases:

- **Detection:** monitoring flags unusual activity, for example a login from an unexpected location or an unusual volume of outbound data.
- **Analysis:** the responders establish what actually happened, which systems are involved and which data is affected.
- **Containment:** the affected systems are isolated so the incident cannot spread further, for instance by removing a host from the network.
- **Eradication:** the cause is removed, such as the malware and the account or vulnerability that allowed it in.
- **Recovery:** the affected systems are restored to normal operation and watched closely for signs that the attacker is still present.
- **Post-incident activity:** the breach is reported to the authorities as required by law, and the lessons learned are fed back into the plan so the same path cannot be used again.

## Incident Response Plans

An Incident Response Plan is a documented, tested, and implemented plan that provides the organization with a structured approach for handling cybersecurity incidents.
Incident Response plans are often tested through exercises known as table-top exercises. These exercises involve simulating a breach and practicing the steps of the incident response plan. They can help identify weaknesses in the plan and ensure that all members know what they have to do in a real scenario.
