# Government Agencies

Every security professional deals with government agencies sooner or later. A company that suffers a ransomware attack may be legally required to report it to a national authority within 24 hours. A penetration tester reads advisories published by a national CERT. A threat-intelligence analyst attributes an attack to a group run by a foreign intelligence service. An incident responder hands evidence to the police. This module maps that landscape: who the agencies are, what each one is responsible for, and where a security professional meets them.

The module is organised around three pillars: cyber defence bodies and CERTs, intelligence services, and law enforcement. It focuses on Austria and Germany and places both in their EU and international context.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish the three pillars of state involvement in cybersecurity and their different mandates
- Name the key cyber defence authorities and CERTs in Austria, Germany and the EU, and describe what they provide
- Explain what NIS2 requires of an affected organisation when it suffers a significant incident
- Describe the role of intelligence services in cyberspace, including signals intelligence, state-attributed threat actors and lawful interception
- Explain how intelligence services are overseen in Austria and Germany
- Describe how cybercrime is investigated across borders, from national police units to Europol, Eurojust and the Budapest Convention

## Topics Covered

1. Introduction: the agency landscape
   - Three pillars and why they are kept separate
   - National, EU and international levels
1. Cyber Defence Bodies and CERTs
   - BSI, CERT.at, GovCERT Austria
   - ENISA, CERT-EU, the CSIRTs network
   - CISA and the NCSC as international reference points
   - NIS2 and reporting obligations
1. Intelligence Services and State Actors
   - Signals intelligence and the Five Eyes
   - The intelligence services of Germany and Austria
   - State-attributed threat actors (APTs)
   - Lawful interception and oversight
1. Law Enforcement and International Cooperation
   - Cybercrime units in Austria and Germany
   - Europol EC3, Eurojust, Interpol
   - The Budapest Convention, MLAT and e-evidence

## Prerequisites

- Module 01 (Ethics and Legal), in particular the criminal-law sections
- Helpful: Module 13 (Blue Teaming, incident response) and Module 23 (Darknet and Tor, for the law-enforcement case studies)

By the end of this module, students should know which agency to turn to in which situation, what obligations an organisation has towards the state after an incident, and how state actors appear both as defenders and as attackers.
